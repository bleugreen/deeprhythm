# v0.8 metrical-level correction research

## Decision

Retain v0.7 as the package default. The tempo-balanced model is a benchmark research candidate, not a general v0.8 upgrade. On canonical held-out folds it raises 4%-tolerance Acc1 by **21.38 points on GiantSteps**, **29.10 points on Ballroom**, and **3.19 points on GTZAN**, but a separate broad-music evaluation found a 1.90-point overall regression. The candidate corrects the demonstrated slow-octave bias while introducing a new fast-octave bias outside the benchmark distribution.

The research candidate remains available at `weights/deeprhythm-v0.8-research.pth` for reproduction. It must not replace v0.7 until a successor clears both the canonical tests and a grouped, contamination-checked broad-music acceptance set.

## Attribution

| Dataset | 0.7 half errors | Fine-tuned half errors | 0.7 correct | Fine-tuned correct |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| GiantSteps | 34 | **2** | 93 | **121** |
| GTZAN | 46 | **31** | 216 | **226** |
| Ballroom | 38 | **5** | 80 | **119** |

A 90-BPM global prior raises combined validation Acc1 from 63.75% to 72.17%, but cannot distinguish resolution from convention. Top-k mass and clip disagreement reach 64.72% and 64.40%; the onset classifier reaches 66.02%. On test the onset model improves 0.7 GiantSteps by 5.34 points but loses 1.28 on GTZAN and 0.75 on Ballroom. Fine-tuning removes half-tempo errors across domains, which is stronger evidence of genuine octave resolution.

Raw fine-tuned confidence remains poorly calibrated: ECE is 0.243 GiantSteps, 0.295 GTZAN, and 0.352 Ballroom.

## Broad-music acceptance check

A post-hoc diagnostic used 578 tracks on which Librosa, Essentia RhythmExtractor2013, and TempoCNN independently agreed at the same octave within 4%. Audio fingerprints did not overlap the canonical test folds. These are high-precision pseudo-references rather than human ground truth, so this check is an acceptance guardrail, not a replacement headline.

| Collection | v0.7 | Research candidate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Bass Club (323) | 95.67% | **95.98%** |
| Other downloads (255) | **95.29%** | 90.59% |
| Overall (578) | **95.50%** | 93.60% |

The regression is decisive for release policy. Two exploratory remedies also failed the acceptance bar. Fine-tuning on grouped consensus pseudo-labels reduced both broad-music and canonical performance. A grouped out-of-fold selector between v0.7 and the candidate preserved 95.50% overall but regressed the non-Bass slice; adding onset-periodicity features scored 94.81% overall. Collection-specific switching would conceal the problem behind a dataset prior and is rejected.

A subsequent DeepRhythm-only preservation experiment used v0.7 distribution matching on unlabeled replay audio alongside canonical human supervision. A validation-selected interpolation exactly matched v0.7 on grouped broad-music validation. On the frozen tests it scored 70.23% GiantSteps, 72.20% GTZAN, 64.18% Ballroom, and tied v0.7 at 93.75% on the 48-track grouped broad-music test. This is 15 additional correct canonical tracks overall, but it loses one GiantSteps track and relies on private replay audio that cannot make a reproducible public checkpoint. The weights are therefore not shipped. The supported training stack retains preservation distillation so the experiment can be repeated with a licensed, manifestable replay corpus.

The two models remain complementary: an oracle choice reaches 96.95% on GiantSteps, 79.55% on GTZAN, 91.04% on Ballroom, and 98.27% on the broad-music check.

A subsequent hierarchical prototype separated a half-BPM base-rate head over 60–120 BPM from a four-class metrical-level head (×½, ×1, ×2, ×4). End-to-end canonical training showed that 13 of 14 broad-validation errors had the correct base rate but the wrong level. Freezing the backbone and base head, then preserving v0.7's level posterior on unlabeled replay clips, produced 87.02% GiantSteps, 69.33% GTZAN, and 91.04% Ballroom on the frozen tests. It scored 91.67% rather than v0.7's 93.75% on the 48-track grouped broad-music test: 64 additional canonical tracks correct at the cost of one broad-test track. This identifies the right architectural boundary but does not clear the no-regression release gate. The checkpoint is not shipped because its replay corpus is private; reproducing the level-head preservation stage requires a licensed, manifestable broad corpus.

## Latency

The fine-tuned network has the same architecture and inference cost as 0.7. On 578 held-out tracks, onset features cost 3.00 ms/track with eight workers. Warm serial cost over 20 preloaded tracks is 2.94 ms mean, 2.91 ms median, and 3.29 ms p95. The recommended model-only design adds no runtime latency.

The subsequently packaged opt-in metrical predictor is a different, two-branch
design. Its production replay over all 798 canonical, Groove, and Slakh test
tracks took 110.17 ms mean, 51.84 ms median, and 357.70 ms p95 per decoded file
on MPS. Cost is track-length dependent because HCQM probabilities are averaged
over every complete eight-second clip after training-compatible edge trimming;
the temporal branch still consumes only the first four clips and reuses the
same nnAudio STFT.

## Packaged v0.8 qualification

The installed `MetricalDeepRhythmPredictor` was replayed from audio rather than
from cached logits. After distilling a stronger beat-rate teacher into the
existing self-contained temporal fusion, it scored 79.76% Acc1 / 94.29% Acc2
on the 578-track canonical union, 77.14% / 98.57% on Groove, and 84.00% /
97.33% on Slakh. Canonical per-dataset Acc1 was 90.08% GiantSteps, 70.29%
GTZAN, and 91.79% Ballroom. Six representative tracks produced identical BPMs
on CPU and MPS.

Distillation changes only the small fusion weights: Phasefinder and the
beat-rate teacher are not runtime dependencies. On untouched human-held-out
audio, CREC improved from 67.39% to 76.09% Acc1 and from 97.83% to 100% Acc2;
Soulseek changed one of 97 predictions and declined from 94.85% to 93.81%
Acc1, with Acc2 unchanged at 100%. This remaining individual-octave tradeoff is
why v0.8 stays opt-in rather than silently replacing the original predictor.
The teacher/student selection and held-out summaries are frozen in
[`distilled-metrical-fusion.json`](results/distilled-metrical-fusion.json).

The complete per-track audit trail, runtime, latencies, and checkpoint hashes
are in [`production-v08-qualification.json`](results/production-v08-qualification.json).

## External acquisition

No new external audio entered training. Extended Ballroom publishes metadata and a downloader but not explicit source-audio training rights. GiantSteps uses Beatport previews without a local training/redistribution grant. The public Hainsworth mirror does not establish a license for copyrighted excerpts. ISMIR04, Harmonix, RWC, and Beatles could not meet both alignment and rights requirements. FMA/MTG-Jamendo pseudo-labeling was not run because the required independent same-octave ensemble was unavailable; DeepRhythm confidence was intentionally not substituted.

Thus +tier-1 and +tier-1+3 ablations are blocked by evidence, not silently omitted. The measured attribution is tempo-balanced canonical-fold fine-tuning, not external data volume.

## Literature

Schreiber and Müller's [octave correction](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2017/paper/000125.pdf) motivates the periodicity reproduction; their [single-step CNN](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2018/paper/000151.pdf) describes sweet-octave behavior. Gkiokas et al.'s [periodicity-vector classifier](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2012/paper/000065.pdf) supports a small reranker, while Böck and Davies' [multi-task model](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2020/paper/000035.pdf) shows why beat structure is stronger evidence than a tempo posterior.

Evaluation follows [Music Tempo Estimation: Are We Done Yet?](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.43). Provenance sources include [GiantSteps](https://github.com/GiantSteps/giantsteps-tempo-dataset), [Extended Ballroom](http://anasynth.ircam.fr/home/media/ExtendedBallroom), and Sturm's [GTZAN analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.1461).

## Reproduction and limitations

Inference details retain per-clip and aggregate peaks plus five candidate masses without changing legacy JSONL. deeprhythm-taxonomy emits taxonomy and calibration. deeprhythm-correction rejects test rows during selection. deeprhythm.train provides manifest-hashed caching, balanced sampling, time-stretch/relabel primitives, and train/validation-only checkpointing.

Time-stretch augmentation is implemented but not included in this frozen checkpoint because augmented manifest rows were not materialized. External composition and augmentation ablations remain future experiments, not implied results.
